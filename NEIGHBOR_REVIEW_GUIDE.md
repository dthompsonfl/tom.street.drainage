# Before you share this with other neighbors

**Tom Street drainage | Neighborhood review copy | October 16, 2026**

I put this folder together so the people who live near Tom Street can look at the same records, understand what we still do not know, and tell me when I have misunderstood something. You do not need a GitHub account, engineering background, or legal knowledge to review it.

**The short version:** I am concerned that grading near new construction might change how rainwater drains around existing homes. County records identify building permits for 2264 and 2268 Tom Street. I have not obtained the approved site-specific grading plans or a field survey showing actual 2026 drainage elevations. This collection is a request for answers, not proof of wrongdoing.

**Edition status:** The existing links below point to **current unsigned draft documents**; they are not one personally approved edition. The [dated map and pinned neighborhood candidate](19_NEIGHBOR_REVIEW/README.md) are technically generated and available for source/visual review through read-only GitHub Actions. No version is authorized for neighborhood distribution yet. You can continue factual discussion using the public source library without treating a draft as a signed notice.

## Review it in about 10 minutes

1. **Read the [homeowner brief](15_RECONCILED_DRAFTS/01_HOMEOWNER_BRIEF.md)** to understand the issue in plain English.
2. **Open the [five-page draft meeting packet](15_RECONCILED_DRAFTS/pdf/Tom_Street_Reconciled_Public_Meeting_Draft.pdf)**. You can read or print it. **It is not a signed or submitted letter.**
3. **Use the [evidence guide](PUBLIC_EVIDENCE_GUIDE.md)** to see which government records support the statements and what those records do not prove.
4. **Look at the [open questions](00_CONTROL/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md)**. We are still waiting for official engineering plans, drainage measurements and maintenance records.
5. **Tell me what is confusing, inaccurate, missing or especially important to your property.** You do not have to agree with my concern to help make the record accurate.

## What I would appreciate you checking

- Does the brief explain the concern without assuming a builder, contractor or the County did something wrong?
- Is anything described as an established fact that you know was only an observation, possibility or inspector's instruction?
- Do any addresses, dates, permit numbers, photographs or descriptions seem inconsistent with what you know?
- Are there other reasonable explanations, earlier drainage problems or repairs we should investigate?
- Do the questions to the County cover what you need to understand?

If you find an error, please give me the **document name, the exact statement and the correction you suggest**, plus an official or firsthand source when possible. It is perfectly acceptable to say, "I don't know whether that is correct."

## If you need to contact the County

These are two different official County processes:

- **Request existing permits, plans or records:** [Santa Rosa County public-records request form](https://www.santarosa.fl.gov/FormCenter/Public-Record-Request-10/Submit-a-Public-Records-Request-55).
- **Report a drainage problem or ask Public Works to investigate:** [Santa Rosa County Citizen Work Request form](https://www.santarosa.fl.gov/FormCenter/Public-Works-7/Citizen-Work-Request-48).

A records request does not automatically schedule a field inspection, and a repair/work request is not the same as receiving approved plans. The draft requests in this repository have **not** been documented as sent. Keep your actual submission confirmation, response, and case number privately. The [request-tracking instructions](18_NEIGHBOR_TOOLKIT/02_REQUESTS_AND_RESPONSES.md) explain how.

## How to contribute without exposing private information

For a **public document or wording correction**, you can use the repository's [public GitHub issue form](https://github.com/dthompsonfl/tom.street.drainage/issues/new/choose) if you already have an account. You can also share feedback privately with the person who gave you this link. No GitHub account is required to read the repository or PDFs.

**Please do not put personal photographs, private emails, exact photo GPS data, neighbors' names or contact details into a public issue.** Keep your originals in private storage. The [neighbor toolkit](18_NEIGHBOR_TOOLKIT/README.md) explains how to document observations and keep a private log.

If you want to use these records for your own proceedings or public-records request, keep a copy of the exact version you relied on and the date you accessed it. Write and sign your own statements based on **your** firsthand knowledge; another resident's letter is not automatically your testimony. The [publication and versioning guide](PUBLICATION_AND_VERSIONING.md) explains how corrections are tracked.

## What this version is, and is not

**Suitable for:** neighbor reading, fact-checking, discussion, recording questions and proposing corrections.

**Not yet approved for:** representing every neighbor, making a verified engineering conclusion, asserting legal violations, publishing private resident evidence, or distributing the draft letters as signed official submissions.

We still need the original approved grading/drainage plans, surveyed grades and pipe inverts, an identified receiving outlet, asset-maintenance documents, and written County findings. The [community readiness register](00_CONTROL/COMMUNITY_READINESS_2026-10-16.md) tracks these limits.

Thank you for helping me keep this accurate. A careful question backed by records will do us more good than a dramatic claim we cannot prove.
