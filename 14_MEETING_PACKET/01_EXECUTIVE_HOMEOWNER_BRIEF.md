## What this is

A factual summary of what Santa Rosa County's own public permit records say
about two new houses being built next door on Tom Street, and what those records
do **not** yet tell us.

We are neighbours, not engineers or lawyers. Nothing here is a legal opinion, a
drainage calculation, or an accusation. Where a question is unanswered, we say
so plainly.

## 2. What county inspectors actually wrote

These are the words of Santa Rosa County inspectors, taken verbatim from the
county permit database.

**2264 Tom Street — driveway review, 6 August 2026 (passed):**

> "Use 100 ft of 12 inch ADS perforated pipe with sock; Tear out existing
> concrete end @ address 2260 and tie into with new pipe; Install 1st inlet box
> @ roughly halfway; Tear out existing concrete end @ address 2270 and install
> an inlet box; call for Pre-pour inspection when ready"

This is the most useful sentence we found. It shows the 2264 driveway was
designed to **connect into drainage structures on the neighbouring lots at 2260
and 2270**, using 12-inch perforated pipe and at least one inlet box.

**2268 Tom Street — pre-construction grading and drainage, 31 August 2026
(passed):**

> "Site passes. Ensure that roadway and stormwater ditch remain free of
> construction sediment."

This confirms the county inspector saw an existing **roadway and stormwater
ditch** along the Tom Street frontage.

**2268 Tom Street — driveway review, 10 September 2026 (passed):**

> "Flat; Match Existing roadway Grades"

The 2268 driveway was directed to be set level and to follow the existing road
surface.

**What these comments are not.** They are an inspector's instructions recorded
during construction. They are **not** an approved engineering drawing, not a
survey, not a measurement of how much freeboard the driveway has, and not proof
of what was actually installed in the ground.

## 3. The most important finding

On **both** permits, the county still has an open task called:

> **"Certified Drainage as Built" — status: Assigned**

Both permits also still show `Site Grading / Drainage (mid-construction)` and
`(final construction)` inspections as **Not Complete**, `Driveway Final
Inspection` as **Not Complete**, and **`Issue CO` as Assigned** — meaning no
Certificate of Occupancy has been issued for either house yet.

**Why that matters.** Santa Rosa County's Land Development Code § 4.04.01.D.1
requires, for a lot under one acre, that the **engineer of record provide a
signed and sealed letter certifying the lot grading plan was built in
conformance** — *at substantial completion and prior to Certificate of
Occupancy*.

In other words: **the sealed engineer's drainage certification does not exist yet
for either lot, and cannot exist until the houses are finished.**

**What this is not.** It is **not** a finding that anything is wrong. Both
permits are Active and this is the ordinary sequence for a house under
construction. It is flagged because it means the final, sealed answer to "was
this lot graded as approved?" has not been produced yet — and that is precisely
the document we want to receive before the houses are finished.

## 4. What we could not obtain

We searched the county's public permit portal. It returns **zero** attached
documents for either permit — no site plan, no grading plan, no survey, no
as-built. This does **not** mean the County lacks them; it means they are not
published.

We have therefore prepared five formal public-records requests. **None has been
sent yet** — that needs the homeowner's authorisation.

The single most important unanswered question:

> **Does the County Engineering Department already have a subdivision lot
> grading plan on file for these two lots?**

That answer decides which legal path the County itself required here — a sealed
engineer's lot grading plan, or an individual Environmental Resource Permit from
Northwest Florida Water Management District. Nobody has answered it yet.

## 5. A note on the neighbours at 2260 and 2270

Because 2264's driveway ties into the drainage structures on those two lots,
their records matter to us. Both of their driveway-connection permits failed
inspection more than once before finally passing — 2260 twice, 2270 three times.
Both were later closed out as complete.

This is **not** evidence of a problem at 2264 or 2268. It is background: it
shows the roadside drainage connections on this stretch of Tom Street have not
always been straightforward.

---

## 6. What we are asking for

1. The **approved grading plan and site plan** for both lots.
2. The **sealed engineer's drainage certification** when it is issued.
3. Confirmation of the **outfall and invert elevations** for each driveway.
4. The **roadway profile** — crown and shoulder elevations — for this block.
5. A **field walk** of the finished drainage by the County and the engineer.
6. A written **maintenance assurance** for the roadside ditch and the shared
   inlet boxes.

Items 1 and 2 are the ones that matter most. Asking for them now, while both
permits are still open, is far more effective than asking after the houses are
finished and the certificates are gone.

## Honest bottom line

We have solid, verifiable county records on **who owns the lots, what permits
are open, what the inspectors wrote, and what the County's code requires.**

We do **not** have the approved grading plans, the surveys, the as-builts, any
field measurements, or any photographs of the water we have seen. Until we do,
**we cannot say whether the drainage at 2264 and 2268 is adequate.** We are not
going to guess, and we are not going to say anything has been done wrong when
the record simply does not tell us yet.

Next step is a short, factual conversation — with the County, and with the
engineer who signs the drainage certification.
