# Proxy Speaking Notes — Tom Street Drainage

**For a neighbour presenting on the homeowner's behalf**
2264 / 2268 Tom Street · Navarre, FL 32566 · prepared 16 October 2026

*You do not need to be an engineer or a lawyer. Speak slowly, stick to what the
records say, and say "not yet known" whenever you are unsure. Credibility is the
whole point.*

---

## Opening (30 seconds)

> "Good evening. I'm speaking for the residents at 2265 Tom Street, next to the
> two new houses at 2264 and 2268. We're not here with a complaint. We're here
> because two building permits are still open, and we'd like to ask three simple
> questions while they're still open."

---

## What we know for certain (say this part confidently)

> "Everything I'm about to say comes from Santa Rosa County's own records — the
> Property Appraiser roll and the county's permit system.

> **Holiday Builders owns both lots.** Two permits are active right now:
> BP-2026-1621 for 2264 Tom Street, and BP-2026-1612 for 2268 Tom Street. Both
> were issued on the 23rd of March, 2026, and both are still Active.

> One correction worth mentioning: the parcel numbers in the property listings
> for these two addresses are **swapped**, and a listing also attaches a deed
> from a different house down the street. The county's official numbers are
> 2264 is 21-2S-26-0780-0QQ00-1020, and 2268 is 21-2S-26-0780-0QQ00-1490."

---

## The three questions

### 1. The driveway ties into the neighbours' drainage

> "On the 6th of August, the county inspector reviewed the driveway at 2264 and
> wrote this — I'm reading it word for word:
>
> *'Use 100 ft of 12 inch ADS perforated pipe with sock; Tear out existing
> concrete end @ address 2260 and tie into with new pipe; Install 1st inlet box
> @ roughly halfway; Tear out existing concrete end @ address 2270 and install
> an inlet box.'*
>
> So the 2264 driveway drains into structures on the 2260 and 2270 properties.
> That means the drainage at 2264 is partly on our neighbours' land."

### 2. There is a stormwater ditch, and the county knows it

> "On the 31st of August, the inspector at 2268 passed the site grading and
> wrote: *'Site passes. Ensure that roadway and stormwater ditch remain free of
> construction sediment.'*
>
> And the driveway at 2268 was directed to be *'Flat; Match Existing roadway
> Grades.'* — set level, following the existing road surface."

**If asked "is that bad?"** →
> "I'm not saying it's bad. I'm saying it's the design that was approved, and
> I'd like to see the drawing that shows the same thing."

### 3. The sealed drainage certification does not exist yet

> "On **both** permits, the county still has an open task called *'Certified
> Drainage as Built'* — status *Assigned*. The final grading inspections are
> *Not Complete*. No Certificate of Occupancy has been issued for either house.

> And the county's own Land Development Code says — section 4.04.01.D.1 — that
> for a lot under an acre, the engineer has to provide a **signed and sealed
> letter certifying the grading was built as approved**, and that this happens
> **before** the Certificate of Occupancy.

> So the document that actually answers our question hasn't been created yet.
> That's precisely why we're asking now."

**If asked "so something is wrong?"** →
> "No. Not at all. Both permits are Active — this is what an unfinished house
> looks like. We're simply asking for the document when it arrives."

---

## The asks — keep it short

> "We're asking the county for four things:
>
> 1. The approved grading plan for each lot.
> 2. The sealed engineer's drainage certification when it's filed.
> 3. The outfall and invert elevations for each driveway.
> 4. A field walk of the finished drainage — and we'd like to be there.

> We're also asking one direct question: **does County Engineering already have
> a subdivision lot grading plan on file for these two lots?** Because the code
> requires one path or the other, and that determines what we're actually
> looking at."

---

## Questions to put to the builder or engineer, if present

1. "Which lot grading plan did the County approve for this lot, and may we see it?"
2. "What is the positive outfall for this driveway, and what are the invert
   elevations?"
3. "Who is the engineer of record who will sign the drainage as-built?"
4. "Does the 2264 driveway connection into the 2260 and 2270 structures have
   anything in writing — a plan or an easement?"
5. "Will the final grading inspection happen before the Certificate of
   Occupancy, and can residents observe?"

## If you are challenged

| Challenge | Response |
|---|---|
| "You're accusing the builder." | "No. Nothing here accuses anyone. These are county records and four polite requests." |
| "You're an engineer?" | "No, and I'm not claiming to be. That's exactly why we want the engineer's sealed letter." |
| "You flooded." | "Have you filed a complaint? Did the county come out? *(if not)* Then let's get the actual records instead of trading descriptions." |
| "The permits say it passed." | "They do — every inspection passed. We're not disputing that. We're asking for the plan behind the passes." |
| "The other driveway did fail inspections." | "Both eventually closed complete. We're not saying anything's wrong — just that these connections have needed rework before." |
| "Flood zone is X, you're fine." | "Zone X is about the mapped floodplain. It doesn't measure whether the street ditch holds water after a storm." |

## Close

> "To be clear about where we stand: we have good county records on who owns
> these lots, what permits are open, and what the inspectors wrote. We do **not**
> have the grading plans, the surveys, the as-builts, or any measurements. So we
> can't tell you today whether the drainage is adequate — and we're not going
> to guess.

> We just want the four documents, and a field walk, before the houses are
> finished. Thank you."

---

**Never say:** that anyone violated the code; that the drainage is inadequate;
that a flood is caused by the build; that the county approved something
dangerous. We have not established any of those, and saying them would cost us
the room.

**Never present** a screenshot of the permit portal, an internal API response,
or anything showing an account holder's name — those are not ours to share.
