# At a County or neighborhood meeting

Bring only what you can explain. The short [homeowner brief](../15_RECONCILED_DRAFTS/01_HOMEOWNER_BRIEF.md) is the starting point; technical records can be supplied afterward.

**Opening:** "I live near the construction on Tom Street. I am concerned about how runoff will drain around existing homes. Can the County identify the plans that were approved and arrange an inspection?"

**Ask for a usable answer:** the responsible Engineering contact, a case number, the specific plan set and revision, a site-review date, the person who maintains the drainage structures and when written findings will be available.

**Keep an accurate record:** write down who attended, each specific promise and the follow-up date. Use [blank meeting notes](templates/MEETING_NOTES.md). Afterward, send a polite summary asking the official to correct anything misunderstood.

**Avoid overclaiming:** don't say a culvert was definitely installed because an inspector recommended it; don't describe a 2017 terrain model as current elevations; and don't say a law was violated when the governing plan and field survey are missing.

A neighbor speaking for someone else should confirm permission and identify whose own observations are being shared. No one needs to share private photographs or personal contact details publicly in order to ask questions.
