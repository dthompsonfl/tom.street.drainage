# Source and measurement guide | Plain English

The difference between a useful observation and a technical conclusion matters. You can document what you can see, but accurate grade, pipe-invert and outlet measurements normally require a qualified survey or engineering review.

## Terms a neighbor may encounter

| Term | In ordinary language |
| --- | --- |
| **Swale / ditch** | A shallow drainage channel intended to carry or temporarily hold water |
| **Inlet** | A point where surface water enters a drain or pipe |
| **Culvert / pipe** | A conduit that may carry water under a road or driveway |
| **Outfall / outlet** | Where a drainage route discharges downstream |
| **Invert elevation** | Measured elevation of the inside bottom of a pipe at a stated point |
| **Grade / slope** | Ground elevation or the rate at which elevation changes |
| **Datum** | The height reference used for elevations; numbers with different datums are not directly comparable |
| **As-built / record drawing** | Documentation intended to describe completed construction, which still requires checking its status and accuracy |
| **Easement** | A recorded right affecting the use or maintenance of a specific piece of land |
| **Runoff** | Rainwater moving over land surfaces |

## What is useful to document firsthand?

- Repeated photographs from **the same viewpoint**, with a recognizable permanent landmark.
- Exact times when rain started or ended and when standing water appeared or disappeared.
- Water direction if directly visible, areas that remained dry and changes following actual work.
- Safe measurements only when you can describe the method, units, reference and uncertainty; an estimate should say "approximate."
- Conditions from before recent construction, whether helpful or contrary to the concern.

## What requires another source?

- **Approved design**: actual approved grading/drainage plan and dated revisions, not a fee entry or a proposed pipe note.
- **Completed work**: inspection, work, survey or acceptance records that show what actually happened; an instruction alone is not completion.
- **Actual elevations**: comparable measurements tied to a stated datum and clearly identified points, not visual impressions or historical terrain maps.
- **Downstream route**: an observed or documented connection, pipe size, inlet, outlet and receiving path; proximity does not establish a connection.
- **Maintenance**: a recorded agreement, asset register or written agency answer for the specific feature.

If two records differ, preserve both, note their issue dates and ask which plan or status controls. An online page with no attachments does not prove the County lacks the plans. It is better to write **unknown** than to fill a gap with an assumption.
