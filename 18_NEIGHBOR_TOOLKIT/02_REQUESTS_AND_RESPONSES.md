# Keep records requests, service reports and replies traceable

A request for **existing public records** is different from a request for an **inspection, service visit or repair**. Keep these as separate entries even if they concern the same location. The project has [agency request drafts](../13_PUBLIC_RECORDS_REQUESTS/) that are currently **not verified as sent**. A saved draft is never a submission receipt.

Start with the [one-page contact form](print/10_Request_and_Response.pdf), packet page 10. If a conversation runs long, attach a [continuation page](print/18_Continuation_Page.pdf).

## Every contact needs a complete trail

1. Write **what was requested** and identify any specific lot, road segment, date range, permit or record type. When requesting existing documents, avoid requiring the office to invent a new measurement or conclusion.
2. Record the **office**, date/time/time zone, delivery method and private contact destination. Keep an unchanged copy of the actual sent message and attachments.
3. Keep the **acknowledgment**, reference or case number and exact response. Log separately what was promised, actually provided, withheld, redacted or still outstanding.
4. Save each delivered file **unchanged** with its filename and source. Give it a record ID (`R-001`) and note the issuing date, version, plan revision and receipt date. Do not silently replace one plan revision with another.
5. Track the **next action** with a person or office and a date. When following up, quote the previous case number and identify the exact missing items. A partial response should be marked partial, not complete.

**Useful questions:** What approved grading and drainage plans, revisions, conditions or written exemptions apply to the relevant permits? Are drainage structures shown in any as-built, inspection, work-order, or maintenance record? Which records identify the feature-by-feature owner and maintainer? Is there a written inspection finding, rather than only a scheduled visit or an inspector's recommendation?

Do not invent a response deadline or describe a document as absent merely because a portal shows no attachments. For a records request, the applicable Florida public-records rules and the agency's current process should be checked directly. A request or service report does not itself prove a drainage issue.
