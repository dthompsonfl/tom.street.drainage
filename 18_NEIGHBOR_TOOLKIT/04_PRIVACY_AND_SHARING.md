# Private evidence versus the public GitHub repository

**Assume anything pushed to GitHub is publicly readable and may remain available in history after deletion.**

Keep these **private** unless you have authority and documented permission to release them: original resident photographs and metadata; home/phone/email details not already authorized for publication; complete email threads; medical or family information; neighbor statements containing identifying details; account credentials, cookies and authenticated portal captures; and unredacted documents received from other people.

Keep originals unchanged in an access-controlled folder owned by the person or coordinator responsible for them. Record the source, acquisition time and SHA-256 if available. Make redacted copies for sharing. Removing visible text on top of an image is **not** necessarily secure redaction; check the actual rendered output and its metadata.

For a proposed public update, provide a safe summary: evidence ID, issuing agency or source, document date, what it says, what it does not prove, how it was obtained and confirmation that a privacy review occurred. Do not add residents' names, exact location metadata or raw private attachments to a public issue.

If something sensitive was accidentally committed, stop circulating the link, preserve incident details privately and assess history exposure. Removing the current file alone is not a reliable privacy fix.
